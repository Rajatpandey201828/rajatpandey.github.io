Research

My current work is on bank capital regulation and the measurement of systemic risk in India. Earlier work looked at monetary policy and equity markets, and at forecasting models for stock prices.

Working paper

Input Conventions in ΔCoVaR: A Frequency Mismatch, Not Leverage, Moves the Ranking

Under reviewWith Amrit Raj (Institute of Rural Management Anand)

Supervisors and researchers rank banks by systemic importance using market-based measures such as ΔCoVaR. Estimating it involves implementation choices that applied studies seldom report. Using Indian banks, this paper asks whether one of those choices changes which banks appear systemically important, and why.

Submitted to the IIM Calcutta Finance Research Conference, December 2026. JEL G21, G28, G32. Draft available on request.

Research in progress

Capital Charges and the Recognition of Household Credit Risk: Bank-Level Evidence from India’s 2023 Consumer-Credit Risk-Weight Increase

In progressSole-authored

In November 2023 the Reserve Bank of India raised the risk weights banks apply to unsecured consumer credit by 25 percentage points, making that lending more expensive in regulatory capital. This project uses the change to study whether higher capital charges alter how banks recognise credit risk in their household loan books. The design uses bank-level panel data with difference-in-differences and event-study estimators.

JEL G21, G28.

Other writing

Stock Price Prediction: A Comprehensive Critical Review of Traditional and Modern Computational Models

SSRN, 2025Sole-authored

A critical survey of 112 empirical studies from 1988 to 2025 across seven model families, from ARIMA and GARCH to Transformers, reinforcement learning and graph neural networks. It finds that deep-learning models win most controlled comparisons, but that the gains are overstated by publication bias, data snooping and the neglect of trading costs. It closes with a model-selection framework and eight directions for future research.

Read the detailed summary Paper on SSRN doi:10.2139/ssrn.5353725

Cash Reserve Ratio and Stock-Market Dynamics: Investigating the Influence on India’s NIFTY-50 Index

2025Master’s research project, Banaras Hindu University

Does the Reserve Bank of India’s cash reserve ratio move the stock market? Using monthly data for 2019 to 2023 and a vector autoregression, the study finds that changes in the CRR Granger-cause the NIFTY 50 but not the reverse. A tightening shock lowers the index for one to three months before the market adjusts.
